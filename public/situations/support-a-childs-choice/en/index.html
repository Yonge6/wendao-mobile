Her Craft Project. Dad's Bid for a Promotion.

The opening joke is built into the scale of Dad's enthusiasm. Doudou stands at the craft table holding one plain sheet of cardboard and announces, "I want to make a spaceship!" Dad answers, "Leave it to me!" beside a box overflowing with scissors, tape, rulers, and other supplies. One panel later, he is bent over the cardboard with a ruler and even a magnifying glass, declaring, "First, optimize the structure." The warm watercolor room, hanging plants, handmade posters, and scattered tools keep the scene cozy, while his exaggerated level of professional concentration turns an ordinary craft assignment into a miniature engineering review.

Dad's takeover becomes much clearer in the middle of the first page. He spreads detailed spaceship drawings across the table and tells Doudou, "Wait until the design is final," while she simply holds up a yellow star and says, "I want to add a star." Soon the cardboard spacecraft has grown huge enough to dominate almost the entire panel, with Dad still adjusting parts and saying, "This needs changing. And that too." The funniest contrast arrives when Doudou settles back with a cookie and asks, "Dad, is your homework still not done?" An orange-and-white cat sleeps peacefully nearby while Dad keeps working in the background, visibly more occupied by the assignment than its supposed owner.

The turning point arrives when Dad finally looks at the oversized spacecraft and asks, "Wait. Whose spaceship is this?" The next page changes the relationship through actions rather than a lecture. In panel 07 he puts the question back to Doudou: "How do you want to make it?" When she points to an angled cardboard piece and explains, "That's for turning corners," he does not correct her; he pauses with tape in hand and lets her idea remain. In the cutting scene, the division of work is equally clear: Doudou handles the scissors while Dad steadies the cardboard and says, "I'll hold it. You cut." His help is still necessary, but it no longer replaces her decisions.

The later panels make authorship visible in small details. Doudou writes "DOUDOU" and a heart directly on the ship while Dad accepts the smaller role described by her line, "You're ground support." A simple wrench symbol appears beside him instead of another claim to the project. During the test flight, Doudou wears the cardboard spaceship and charges across the room shouting, "Captain, taking off!" Dad is down on the carpet laughing as she leads the play, and the orange-and-white cat remains calmly nearby. The handmade ship is still oversized and imperfect, but those qualities now support the joke: it finally looks like something a child can proudly inhabit rather than something an adult is trying to perfect.

The final classroom scene completes that shift. The spacecraft sits on a display table under the signs "OUR LITTLE INVENTIONS" and "LITTLE MAKERS," with "DOUDOU" still visible on its side as other children gather in front. Doudou stands beside the work presenting it, while Dad stays back in the doorway, gives a thumbs-up, and says, "This time, I'm not the star." The connection to the supplied Daodejing passage is therefore a contemporary reflection rather than a literal retelling: the comic imagines support that nurtures without claiming ownership and guidance that does not become control. Its closing line, "Support small hands. You don't have to choose their direction," turns that idea into the gentle visual punch line of Dad's changed position.

Bring them forth and nurture them; bring them forth without possessing them, foster them without ruling them—this is called profound virtue.

A little room for today

The next time you make, build, or solve something with someone else, you might begin with one question: "How do you want to do it?" Offer a steady hand, a tool, or practical help when it is needed, while leaving the final shape and choices with the person whose project it is.
