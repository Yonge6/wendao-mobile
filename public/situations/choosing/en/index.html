My Bookmarks Have a PhD. I Haven't Started.

The opening turns collecting into a full academic career. The orange-sweater young man sits happily between towers labeled STUDY, INSPIRATION, COURSES, READING, INDUSTRY, TOOLS, and LIFE, while his notebook lies completely blank in the foreground. He confidently says, "Into the Master Learning System it goes," then upgrades the filing ritual with names such as 7-DAY MASTERY, EXPERT SECRETS, ULTIMATE LEARNING, and MENTAL MODELS. Graduation caps perched above the folders make the joke visual before anyone explains it: his resources already look ready for commencement, while he has not yet written a first line.

The comedy escalates because organizing becomes another thing to collect. In panel 03 he thinks, "I'll start practicing once I've organized the methods," while the notebook itself answers silently with "STILL BLANK." Then a tablet offers ORGANIZE YOUR COLLECTION, promising a path "FROM CHAOS TO A SYSTEM" with SORT, MAINTAIN, and USE YOUR KNOWLEDGE beside a bright BUY NOW button. His delighted reaction, "Finish this and I'll be complete!", captures the loop perfectly: preparation for action has produced yet another purchasable layer of preparation.

The blue-shirted, short-haired woman supplies the turning point without changing the room or removing any folders. She points directly at the empty practice book and says, "The practice book's still empty. Your folders have more degrees than you." Beside them, the tower now literally climbs through ELEMENTARY TO-READ, DIPLOMA INSPIRATION, BACHELOR'S RESOURCES, MASTER'S COURSES, and PhD BOOKMARKS, complete with a graduation cap. The young man looks up at this absurd academic monument and admits, "...Accurate. Have I majored in Getting Ready to Start?" The joke works because the diagnosis is already sitting on the desk in plain sight.

The lower page changes the sequence of actions rather than staging a dramatic purge. He says, "No more life upgrades in the cart today," closes the purchase page, and reaches for a PRACTICAL ACTION GUIDE already beside him. Next he sets a visible 10:00 timer and writes in the notebook. His first diagram is rough, surrounded by arrows, boxes, and crumpled paper, but the woman smiles as he jokes, "Crooked—but at least there's finally something to be crooked!" When he reaches a genuinely uncertain step, he consults the guide and writes "REVISE USING THE ANSWER," using information to solve a live problem instead of postponing practice until everything is organized.

By panel 11, the difference is small but concrete: the dated page shows checks beside DRAW THE STRUCTURE, LIST KEY POINTS, and WRITE NEXT STEP, while KEEP PRACTICING remains unfinished. His line, "Ten minutes. Finally, not ten new bookmarks," makes the scale of the victory part of the joke. In the final panel, the towering PhD BOOKMARKS and other degree-labeled folders are still there; nothing has been magically simplified. He and the woman simply sit with their mugs, the worked notebook open in front of them, as he says, "The resources are sky-high. I'll start on the ground." Read alongside "With less, one gains; with more, one becomes confused," the comic offers one contemporary reflection: when options multiply, choosing one sufficient resource and acting on it can make the next step easier to see. It is not presented as the only possible meaning of the ancient line.

A little room for today

You might choose just one saved resource that is useful enough for today, set a ten-minute timer, and make one small attempt. Mark anything you genuinely do not know, then look it up only when that question appears. The goal is not to organize the whole archive first, but simply to leave one real trace of practice on the page.
