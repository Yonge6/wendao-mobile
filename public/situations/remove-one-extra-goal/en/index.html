My Plan Grew Three Kinds of Procrastination

Read the comic reflection

The opening turns an ordinary burst of motivation into a visual joke. The woman in blue announces, "Learn a language! Get fit! Start a channel! Finish my reading list! Starting today!" while her notebook repeats the four-item plan and the black cat watches from beside the desk. In the next panel, the plan has literally grown into a tree: "Language," "Fitness," "Channel," and "Reading list" hang among a clock, camera, books, globe, and other objects. Her delighted "Wow... My plan is growing! So motivating!" captures the funny moment when organizing a future self can already feel like progress.

Panels 03 and 04 show why that excitement becomes heavy. The woman tries to schedule exercise, video work, reading, and language into one packed day while eating at her laptop and handling her phone. Beside her, stacks labeled "Exercise log," "Video script," "Reading list," and "Language notes" rise like physical evidence of productivity. By night she is asleep across the desk next to the black cat, while a clock above the tree appears to melt downward. Her line, "A whole day gone... I still haven't spoken a word. So busy. Still not started," makes the central joke sharper: she has done a great deal around the goals without yet doing the small action she cared about most.

The turning point begins when the man in the red scarf does not offer another elaborate system. He looks at the crowded goal tree and says, "You hung every goal up there. Now you're stuck to the tree. Maybe trim one?" The woman first resists because she worries that choosing less will make her "less well-rounded." In Panel 06 she physically holds the "WELL-ROUNDED" card and asks whether she added it mainly to look impressive. On the lower page, that question becomes a decision: after the man suggests setting aside a goal that is mostly there for appearances, she reaches up and removes the "Channel" tag. The tree remains standing, and the other three goals remain visible.

The space created by that choice immediately becomes concrete rather than heroic. Wearing headphones, the woman speaks to the teacher on her laptop: "Hi! Nice to meet you! I'm nervous, but I finally spoke. Not as scary as I thought!" The teacher on screen answers, "Nice to meet you!" and her notebook records the modest achievement "Today: Spoke once." In the next panel she stretches beside the black cat while her checklist shows language practice and thirty minutes of exercise checked off, with the reading list still honestly marked "in progress." Nothing in the image suggests that she has mastered everything; the comedy and relief come from two real actions finally replacing a mountain of preparation.

The ending keeps the trade-off gentle rather than absolute. The man says that one less goal leaves more room for what she actually wants, and the woman replies, "Setting it aside wasn't failure. It made space for what matters." In the final panel she sits peacefully with a mug and the black cat beneath the tree, looking toward a bright city skyline. Only "Language," "Fitness," and "Reading list" remain hanging beside the softly distorted clock, and she says, "One less wish hanging on it. Now the tree can grow." The closing line, "Some wishes can wait. Let the ones that matter begin," connects naturally with Chapter 42's paradox of loss and gain without claiming that everyone should always pursue fewer goals; here, this particular character benefits from distinguishing what matters now from what can wait.

Some things are increased by being diminished; increased, they may instead be diminished.

A little room for today

Look at today's task list and notice whether one item is there mainly to make the plan look more complete rather than because it needs your attention now. Consider setting that one aside for ten minutes, then use the space for one small beginning you genuinely want: say one sentence in another language, take a short walk, or read one page with full attention.
