I Tried Helping Everyone. Then I Needed Help.

Read the comic reflection

The opening page turns the helper's casual willingness into an increasingly elaborate all-in-one service. In panel 01, the curly-haired helper in the orange-red patterned shirt cheerfully answers a request to help with a Saturday move while a striped cat naps nearby. By panel 02, the helper is already at a laptop with a stack marked "RESUME," agreeing to edit it that night. Panel 03 adds a morning pickup while the helper is carrying a moving box beside a scooter and helmet. None of these favors looks enormous on its own. The comedy arrives in panel 04, when "A bit earlier?", "Resume needed today!", and "Still a dozen boxes here!" appear together around moving boxes, a clock, a resume pile, and a "TO DO" list. The helper's "What? All at once?" simply says what the crowded picture has already made obvious.

The first real turning point comes after the helper stops moving. In panel 05, the backpack, helmet, scattered resume pages, moving boxes, and spotted cat are still physically present, but the helper is sitting on a box with head in hand, finally asking, "Did I ... promise a little too much?" The friend in the blue patterned outfit does not lecture. Instead, in panel 06, the friend offers a bottle of water and says, "You're kind. You're not municipal infrastructure." The helper's small reply, "...Fair point," lets the tension collapse into a joke. The line works because it does not argue against helping people; it pokes fun at the idea that one person should function like an always-available public utility.

Page two shows the helper becoming more specific rather than less caring. In panel 07, the friend says, "Don't promise everything. Say what you CAN do," while the helper drinks water beside two notebooks. One page is filled with repeated "SORRY" entries crossed out in red; the other is headed "WHAT I CAN DO" and lists "Moving 2 hours," "Resume edit," and "No pickup this time." Panel 08 puts that new boundary into an actual message: "I can help move 8–10 AM. I really can't do the pickup." The reply is strikingly uneventful: "Got it. I'll find another ride." With moving boxes and the striped cat still in view, the helper answers, "Huh. Being direct works." The feared confrontation never appears; the practical arrangement simply becomes clearer.

Once the commitments are smaller, the help on the page becomes more concrete. In panel 09, the helper and friend sit together at the laptop, surrounded by papers, a pencil cup, and the visible "RESUME" stack. The helper promises one revision focused on the most urgent parts, and the friend jokes that a paragraph "finally sounds human!" In panel 10, another request arrives for a mock interview. This time the helper does not improvise expertise, replying, "Not my strength. I'll send a reliable career adviser." A "CAREER ADVISER REFERRAL" card makes the alternative visible, and the friend replies, "Better than forcing yourself." Panel 11 applies the same principle to the move: the helper carries a box marked "BOOKS-LIGHT" while "KITCHEN," "BOOKS," and "CLOTHES" boxes remain around them, the clock shows ten, and the friend gives a thumbs-up: "Plenty. And very useful!"

The ending returns the story to ordinary friendship instead of heroic overextension. In panel 12, the helper has the backpack on and is ready to leave, the friend stands by the doorway, the spotted cat sits nearby, and the outdoor sun and buildings restore the relaxed everyday atmosphere. The friend says, "Leave some energy. Kindness lasts longer," and the helper answers with the story's final joke: "Got it. Kindness needs battery life too." The footer, "Skip being infrastructure today. Still be a friend tomorrow," brings the municipal-infrastructure joke full circle. As a contemporary reflection on Chapter 67, the comic loosely connects willingness to help with compassion, preserving some capacity with frugality or restraint, and declining to seize every responsibility with "not daring to be first in the world." It presents that connection as a modern-life association, not as a literal translation of the chapter.

The first is compassion; the second is frugality; the third is not daring to be first in the world.

A little room for today

Before saying yes next time, try stating three things clearly: what you can do, how long you can do it, and which part needs someone else to handle. Like the helper crossing out a page of repeated "SORRY" and replacing it with "WHAT I CAN DO," a truthful, workable range can be both useful and kind.
