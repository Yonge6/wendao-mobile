The Latte Gets Likes. The Foundations Don’t.

Wendao editorial · AI-assisted editing

Save complete comic ↓

Read the comic reflection

The opening joke is wonderfully concrete: the woman in the green apron bends over a carefully poured latte with her phone held like a second piece of café equipment. The chalkboard announces the new Mountain Latte, and she declares, "Gorgeous! This latte art needs a post!" In the next panel she is still searching for better light and predicting that the drink will go viral. The composition makes the contrast visible before anyone explains it: the latte is polished and camera-ready, while the everyday machinery of the café is already beginning to strain around it.

That strain becomes explicit when the bearded man in the brown apron appears beside stacks of cups and warns, "Running low on cups and milk. Restock now?" She answers, "Wait! A few more photos. I’ll post first!" The humor is gentle because nobody is behaving like a villain; they are simply looking at different layers of the same job. One person is focused on the exciting new product, while the other is noticing the supplies that allow any product to reach a customer at all.

The turning point arrives through props rather than philosophy. Customers ask whether the new latte is still available and why the wait is taking so long; the woman realizes they are out of cups and tries to improvise with other mugs. Then a cup and liquid are visibly spilled across the floor, a bright yellow "CAUTION WET FLOOR" sign appears, and she warns that the floor is slippery. By panel 06, the bearded coworker is mopping while offering to clean and restock, and she admits that being absorbed in the photos made her miss what was happening around her. The polished launch has literally met the floor.

The lower page turns that embarrassment into cooperation. In panel 07 he washes and restocks cups while she agrees to sort order priorities. In panel 08 they make the invisible work visible on a large TODAY board: cups, stock, cleaning, orders and drinks, and photos when quiet. Panel 09 shows labeled containers for beans, milk, and syrup while the rush-hour menu is simplified. By panel 10 she can pour another latte and joke, "Enough cups. Clean counter. Nobody panicking!" The chalkboard reading "THREE DRINKS IN PROGRESS" is funny precisely because ordinary stability has become an achievement worth announcing.

The ending quietly reverses the opening idea of what deserves attention. Only after the workflow settles does the woman return to photographing the latte, now saying that it looks good and tastes consistent and is therefore worth sharing; behind her, the board says "NEW DRINKS THIS AFTERNOON." In the final panel, the glamorous latte disappears and everyone raises plain white mugs instead. Her line, "Today’s star: a day with no disasters!" turns the earlier hunger for visibility into a joke about dependable basics. The supplied Daodejing line can be read alongside this as one contemporary association: what is elevated depends on what is lower and less celebrated. The comic does not present that as the only meaning of the passage; it simply lets a café make the relationship visible.

Therefore, what is noble must take what is humble as its root, and what is high must take what is low as its foundation.

A little room for today

Choose one thing you are eager to make impressive and look underneath it for one unglamorous supporting step: restocking, checking, cleaning, confirming, simplifying, or leaving enough time. Try strengthening that layer first, without rushing to display the result, and notice whether the visible work becomes easier to sustain.
