My Smart Home Went on Strike. I Found a Switch.

Wendao editorial · AI-assisted editing

Save complete comic ↓

Read the comic reflection

The opening makes Xin's confidence almost as visible as the technology itself. In the blue-and-gold space-age apartment, he stands beside a sleek control panel labeled LIGHTS, CURTAINS, FAN and ALL CONNECTED, while the futuristic city glows outside. He announces that everything is linked, and the woman answers, "Just say the word? Brilliant!" In the next panel he is already reclining with a mug, pleased that he can "Run the house without getting up." The relaxed pose, polished interface and optimistic "The future!" make the later failure funnier because the comic first lets the convenience look completely convincing.

The joke turns sharply when the apartment goes dark. A large screen reports CONNECTION FAILED and CHECK NETWORK while Xin stares at his phone in alarm. The next panel makes the problem physical and ridiculous: he stands alone in the darkness with his hands around his mouth, repeatedly shouting "Lights on!" into the room. The caption confirms that he does this ten times, but nothing changes. By panel 05 he has collapsed onto the floor and admits, "I've been giving the air a speech." The woman, laughing beside him, replies, "Impressive delivery, though!" The humor comes from effort increasing while the result remains exactly zero.

The turning point is deliberately small. Instead of arguing about whether smart homes are good or bad, the woman brings out a glowing old lamp and a manual switch and says, "Old lamp tonight. Add a manual switch. Get the room usable, then troubleshoot." Xin initially calls the switch backward, but immediately adds that light matters more right now. On the lower page, the warm lamp is already working, and Xin installs the manual switch while the woman watches. His line, "No internet needed. Press it: light," is almost comically plain after the earlier futuristic promises, yet that plainness is precisely what changes the situation.

Once the room is usable again, the comic returns to technology rather than rejecting it. Xin opens a laptop to check the router and smart hub, while a neat stack of books is labeled SMART HOME, NETWORK SETTINGS, TROUBLESHOOTING and BACKUP PLAN. The woman says they can keep the technology, "Just not all our eggs in one basket." The system is eventually restored, and the lights, curtains and fan respond again. In the next relaxed scene, Xin leans back on the sofa while the woman holds a cat, visually showing that the crisis has passed. Convenience has returned, but it now sits beside a backup rather than replacing one.

The final two panels make that change visible. Xin points to a wall display that keeps the manual switch and smart controls side by side: MANUAL / WORKS OFFLINE, SMART / CONVENIENT ONLINE, followed by LIGHTS, CURTAINS and FAN and the line TWO WAYS. LESS WORRY. He says, "Keep the switch. Keep the smart system. Two ways to get light," and the woman answers, "Now that's smart living!" In the closing panel they sit with the cat and look over the glowing futuristic city while the old lamp shines beside them. Xin says that returning to a switch is not the end of progress but a move closer to what they need. Read alongside "Reversal is the movement of the Way," this can be taken as one modest everyday connection: moving forward may sometimes include returning to a neglected basic function. The comic does not present that as the only meaning of the line; it simply uses one failed network and one reachable switch to make the idea concrete.

A little room for today

Choose one convenience you rely on and imagine it unavailable for an evening. Is there a simple backup that would keep the essential function working? You might add one practical alternative, then keep enjoying the smarter system without turning old and new methods into a contest.
