No News Today. Can We Still Meet?

Read the comic reflection

The opening joke is visible right away: Qing sits at the sunlit café table with her laptop, notebook and pen, worrying that she has "nothing new to talk about." Instead of leaving the meeting alone, she turns it into preparation work. In the next panel she writes a full TOPICS list—last week's exhibition, a new café, riverside flowers, a friend's story and a recent film—while telling herself to "prepare a briefing" and "avoid dead air." Qiu's message creates the contrast: "Long time! Coffee Saturday afternoon? No prep. Just a chat." One friend is building a miniature presentation; the other is simply inviting her to show up.

At the riverside café, the plan initially seems to work. Qing cheerfully announces, "I did my homework. Lots to talk about!" while Qiu, resting her face in her hands across the table, replies that she "just wanted to see" her. Qing then starts exactly where her list told her to start: "First, last week's exhibition. Really interesting..." Qiu listens and asks, "Mm-hmm! And then?" The visual rhythm makes that innocent question funny, because the next panel immediately announces "TEN SECONDS OF QUIET" and Qing is already holding her phone, searching for "more material" from the trending list.

The real turning point comes without a lecture. Qiu notices the phone, asks, "Already checking your phone?" and then gently touches Qing's arm while saying, "I invited you, not the trending list." Qing's small "Oh..." is enough. The comic does not turn Qiu into a scolding friend or Qing into a problem to be fixed; the gesture stays affectionate. In the following panel, when Qing asks, "Then... what do we talk about?" Qiu simply answers, "Look at the light on the water for a bit." The story physically moves away from the table and its prepared material toward the riverside itself.

By the railing, the silence starts producing its own kind of conversation. Qing begins, "I was going to say... never mind..." and is allowed to leave the sentence unfinished. Qiu looks at the bright reflections and jokes that the shimmer resembles "coffee spilled across the river." Later, both women laugh while sharing cake, and Qing admits, "I even made an agenda for us." Qiu's reply—"Lucky it wasn't a slide deck"—lands because the comic has already shown how seriously Qing prepared. The humor stays warm: the agenda is gently teased, then replaced by food, observation and company rather than by another supposedly better technique.

The final two panels make the change concrete rather than dramatic. Sitting together again, Qing admits that during the quiet moment she nearly reached for her phone, and Qiu answers, "But you stayed. Nicely done." At sunset, with both women facing each other beside the glowing river, Qing says, "Not much content today." Qiu replies, "Plenty of you, though." The closing line—"The trending list didn't show up. An old friend did."—collects the whole visual journey into one image: the laptop, checklist and phone have faded from importance, while the two friends, the river light, floral cups, daisies and shared cake remain. This connects loosely with the supplied passage's image of "blandness": not as a rule for friendship, but as a contemporary reflection on how something quiet and apparently uneventful can still feel complete.

Thus, when the Way is expressed in words, it is said: bland, ah, without flavor.

A little room for today

Next time you meet someone familiar, try leaving one "must-discuss" topic unprepared. If a few quiet seconds appear, you might wait before reaching for your phone. Look outside, take a sip, or mention one small thing actually in front of you, and see what the conversation does next.
