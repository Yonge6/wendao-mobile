Read the comic reflection

The opening makes the scale of his ambition part of the joke. Sitting at the table with The Meaning of Life, the man announces, "Tonight, I'm going to figure out life." Only three minutes later, his posture has collapsed into his hand and a question mark hangs above him: "I know every word. Still don't understand life." The room, book, mug, lamp and nighttime skyline barely change; what changes is the distance between recognizing words and understanding what they are trying to say.

Instead of staying with that difficulty, he builds an impressive support system around it. The next panel fills the table with Intro to Philosophy, Western Philosophy, Key Concepts Dictionary, Multiple Perspectives, Classics Guide, Cultural Context and Evolution, Modern Thinkers and Interviews with Thinkers. His punch line is that if one book is unclear, he clearly has not "BOUGHT" enough books. Then the colored highlighters appear, covering the open pages so thoroughly that the woman asks whether he is reading the book or decorating it. The humor comes from how productive all this preparation looks while the original confusion remains untouched.

The book-club scene changes the direction without pretending the problem has vanished. In front of the CITY BOOK CLUB sign, the man arrives carrying his stack and holds up a sheet marked "PROFOUND," declaring, "This is... profound." The woman simply asks, "Which line, exactly?" In the next panel the performance drops: the paper now carries a large question mark, and he admits, "This line. I don't understand it." Her answer, "Good. Now we have somewhere to start," turns vagueness into one concrete place they can actually examine together.

The mango sequence gives that distinction a physical joke. The package announces "SWEET," while a real mango and bright slices sit on the plate. The woman asks whether he has actually tasted it; in the following panel his tightly squeezed eyes and puckered, sour face answer more convincingly than the label ever could. Her line, "Sweet label. Honest face," keeps the point playful: a word can indicate something, but the word is not the whole encounter. The comic then returns to the open book, where a hand writes "What does this line mean?" on a sticky note while the woman suggests leaving one question for today.

The ending deliberately avoids instant enlightenment. In the kitchen, the man cuts mango for dinner and says, "Dinner doesn't have to wait for all the answers." A week later he is back at the same book, with the mango bowl nearby and a question-mark bookmark still sticking out of The Meaning of Life. He says only, "I think I understand a little more this time." The closing line, "Leave a question open. Give yourself a reason to turn the page," fits the visible change: not total mastery, but a willingness to return. As a contemporary reflection on Chapter 1, the story echoes the tension between what can be named or said and what remains larger than the name, without treating the comic as a literal translation of the ancient text.

A Dao that can be spoken of is not the constant Dao.

A little room for today

Choose one line you genuinely do not understand and write down one specific question about it. First say, in your own words, what you think you read; then test that reading against one ordinary example. You do not have to settle the question today. Leave it visible and return to it later.
