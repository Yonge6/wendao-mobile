Three Shortcuts Bought. Zero Steps Taken.

Read the comic reflection

The opening joke is how convincingly Jie has prepared to look like a guitarist before becoming one. In the warm brass-and-copper workshop, he sits beside a laptop, a mug marked "MUSIC CHANGES LIFE," and a tower of books labeled "SEVEN-DAY GUITAR," "UNIVERSAL CHORD GUIDE," and "ADVANCED GEAR." By panel 02 he is proudly holding a pristine acoustic guitar and announcing, "Premium strings too. Better tone! Gear ready. Halfway to expert!" The side caption, "STRINGS SO NEW THEY NEED A BIRTH CERTIFICATE," makes the contradiction visible: everything is impressively ready except the playing.

The woman in the green jacket punctures that illusion without turning the scene into a lecture. She simply asks Jie for a song because she has not heard him play in ages. His startled "Now?" is the first crack in the performance of preparedness. Instead of playing, he opens his course plan and explains, "First, my learning system! Basics to advanced. Scientific!" Then he presents the books again and promises universal chords, better gear, effects, and faster progress. Her calm question, "Impressive. Have you started?" turns all those resources from proof of progress into evidence that the real first step is still waiting.

The second page changes the comedy by letting Jie become a beginner in public. He finally sits with the guitar, a notebook listing "POSTURE," "CHORD," "FINGERING," "RHYTHM," and "SHORT PASSAGE" beside him, and tries something small. His fingers immediately refuse to cooperate; he winces over aching hands and "strange noises" while a mug nearby reads "SLOWER CAN BE FASTER." The woman does not push him toward the advanced material. She tells him to slow down and get one clean chord. In the next panel his playing is still rough, but Jie can say, "Not smooth yet. Better than before," and she can genuinely answer, "Progress! I can hear a tune!"

The comic does not throw away methods or learning resources once practice begins. In panel 10, Jie and the woman sit together at the laptop, now using lessons for specific needs such as fingering techniques, rhythm practice, favorite songs, and song playthroughs. The books beside them have also changed function: "BASICS," "LEARN AS NEEDED," "PRACTICE," and "ENJOY MUSIC" read less like a fantasy of instant expertise and more like tools available when a real problem appears. The turning point is therefore not anti-course or anti-efficiency; it is the shift from collecting a route in advance to consulting the map while actually moving.

The ending makes progress physical. Jie proudly holds up a guitar whose body now carries visible scuffs and scratches, calling them "Evidence of actual practice!" The woman jokes that it looks more like his guitar than when it lived on the stand. In the final panel, Jie relaxes with the instrument across his lap and says, "This time the progress bar's in my hands." On the table, "PLAYED A LITTLE TODAY" is checked while "RELAX MORE" and "CONTINUE TOMORROW" remain for later, and the closing banner reads, "Maybe not the fastest route. But this step is real." That gives the supplied Daodejing line a contemporary echo: the attractive shortcut is easy to admire, while the ordinary road only becomes a road when someone actually walks it.

The great Way is very level, yet people greatly prefer bypaths.

A little room for today

Choose one thing you have been mostly "preparing" to do and complete one basic action that takes ten minutes or less. If you get stuck, look up only the method that answers that specific problem, then return to the activity. See whether today can leave one real trace of use, practice, or making instead of one more saved resource.
